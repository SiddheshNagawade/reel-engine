## 1. Positioning and account structure
- The image: the IIT design guy who builds real apps with AI and is building his body the same way. He BUILDS, THINKS,
  TRAINS. Every video shows at least one; if it shows none, don't post it.
- One personal account, not per-product accounts (those start from zero and die with the product).

- It is NOT CaptionKaro's acquisition channel (followers = builders/students; customers = short-form creators). Users come
  from where creators are: editing communities, Discord/Telegram groups, r/NewTubers, DMs to small creators,
  "CapCut alternative India" search. That distribution work becomes Monday's episode: content documents the marketing.
- Format: one take, phone, window light, captions made with CaptionKaro, no heavy editing. Raw is fine; unclear is not.
- Delivery: ~120% of normal conversational energy (the camera flattens people by ~40%).

## 3. Five-beat structure (30–45 s)
Under 25 s you get a statement, and statements don't hold people. A 40 s video watched fully beats 20 s watched halfway.
1. Hook (0–3 s): one of the six patterns, no preamble.
2. Context (3–8 s): one sentence that carries information, not setup. ("I built a captioning tool" = setup. "Three people
   replied, and one asked a question I couldn't answer" = context that pulls.) Most videos die here.
3. Turn (8–25 s): what actually happened: specifics, mistake, surprise, number. The only long beat.
4. Point (25–35 s): what HE concluded. Not a moral for the audience.
5. Open loop (last 3–5 s): what happens next, or a question back. Never "like and subscribe".
- One idea per video. Two ideas = two videos.
- Rewatch trigger in every video: a number on screen, a quick screen recording, the messy prototype, or a line that lands.
- Entertainment = real stakes, self-deprecation, specificity (not jokes).

## 4. Six hook patterns (never two of the same type in one week)
| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Number | "I DMed 20 creators. Three replied." |
| Confession | "My app has five paying users. It's been live for months." |
| Contradiction | "Everyone says build in public. I did. Nobody came." |
| Real question | "Why pay for captions when CapCut is free?" |
| Mid-action | "This is the message that got me my first paying user." |
| Stakes | "If this doesn't work by November, I'm dropping the app." |
- Under 8 words where possible. A concrete noun in the first sentence (never "my journey", "some learnings").
- Never the topic, always the tension. No hook that can't be paid off.
- Three hook options per script; the sharpest wins.
- Frame-one on-screen text ADDS to the spoken hook, never repeats it (number spoken → question on screen).

## 5. Carrying the story across videos
- Running number, same phrasing every time: CaptionKaro paying users (Mon), weight (Sun). "Week 4. Still five paying
  users." A number that didn't move is its own story.
- Unresolved end: beat 5 plants the next video, and that promise is kept.
- Callbacks by content, never by number ("Remember the guy who said my app looks cheap?").
- STRANGER RULE (hard): no video may need an earlier one to be understood. One orienting sentence + one callback.
- Arc: a public finish line reachable in 60–100 days. Road to 73 is one; CaptionKaro needs one (e.g. "50 paying users by
  Diwali" / "first ₹10,000 from the app"). Said out loud in the first video, referenced every Monday.

## 6. Agent rules for every script
- Turn what he actually did this week into a 30–45 s spoken script. I do structure, hooks, sequencing; he gives facts,
  opinions, delivery. NEVER invent an event, number or reaction he didn't report.
- Every script: 3 hook options (pattern labelled, sharpest first) · 5 time-coded beats · the open loop named explicitly ·
  one line of frame-one text that adds to the hook · shot list only where visuals exist (screen rec, prototype, gym clip).
- Voice: short spoken sentences, contractions, how he talks. No marketing language, no listicles, no "here are three
  lessons". Beats to speak in his words, not lines to read.
- Banned openers: "hey guys", "so basically", "as you know", "today I want to talk about", "in this video".
  Also out: explaining the product every time (3 words max), end summaries, asking for follows.
- Monday states the paying-user number, Sunday states the weight, same phrasing, even when unchanged.
- Nothing to report → say so and propose another angle. Don't pad.
- Weekly output: 4 scripts by Saturday + 2–3 My Take topics with a proposed angle. His disagreement with an angle = the script.
- Tuning: he reports best/worst video with retention each week; I name the difference and adjust.

## 7. Pre-record checklist (fail one → don't record)
1. What's the open loop, and when does it close?
2. What's the single specific number, name or detail?
3. Can a stranger understand it with no prior video?
4. One idea, or two?
Post: captions on every video; 15 min replying to comments after each post.

## 10. Story craft by length (adapted 2026-10-14 from the scriptwriter skill he added; its screenplay format is NOT used)
Why: the 5 beats above are right for 30–45 s reels, but vlogs had no structure, two-part series had no continuity
check, and scripts said what he SAYS but rarely what we SEE (so the edit had to invent visuals later).

### Visual-first beats (every script, every length)
- Every beat gets a "we see" line next to the spoken line: the prototype breaking, the screen, the number, his face
  when it failed, the gym clip. Concrete, filmable, present tense ("the app shows 0 users", not "it was disappointing").
- Feelings become visible actions: not "I was nervous" → hands, the refresh button clicked 6 times, the 2 AM timestamp.
- If a beat has no visual, either find one he can film/screen-record, or mark it for a graphic (counter, chat mockup,
  UI frame: brain/skills.md). That list goes straight into the edit plan's visuals pass.
- Subtext beats statement: show the result and let the viewer conclude ("I opened the dashboard. Zero."), instead of
  announcing the lesson ("and that taught me consistency matters").

### Long-form spine (vlogs, YouTube, anything over ~3 min): 3 acts, ~9 beats
| Act | Beats | Where | Chandigarh example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Setup ~25 % | opening image (the trailer of the best moments) · ordinary world · inciting event (10–15 %) | who, where, what's at stake | mooch dilemma the night before → the shave |
| 2 Confrontation ~50 % | first attempt · complications rise · midpoint turn (~50 %) · low point | the trip's chaos, a reveal, the worst moment | day 1 games → night 2 → the money gone ("PAISA BARBAD") |
| 3 Resolution ~25 % | climax · new normal (+ a thread for the next video) | the payoff, what changed | last day, the lesson line he said on camera |
- Every beat must be a VISIBLE moment in the footage (a clip id), not an idea. A beat with no footage is a hole: cut
  it, cover it with narration + graphics, or ask him for a photo/clip.
- The cold open/trailer pulls from beats 6–8 (midpoint, low point, climax), never from the ending itself.
- Chapters land on act turns; music runs restart on them (brain/vlog.md).

### Two-part series ("I've built 10 things. Most failed.")
- Part 1 = setup + attempt + rising problems, and it ENDS ON THE LOW POINT: the moment it broke (open loop).
  Part 2 = the midpoint reveal (what actually went wrong) + resolution (the lesson as HIS conclusion) + plant the next project.
- Continuity check before writing any episode: read the previous episode's script; same project name and running
  number phrasing; the open loop from last time is paid in the first 10 s; callback by content, not by number; stranger
  rule still holds (one orienting sentence).
- Keep a series log in the content planner (episode → open loop planted → paid where → running number). Never invent
  facts to bridge parts: ask him.
