# Core principles: reels people watch to the end

Study notes. Goal of every edit: **the viewer never has a reason to scroll.** Every second must either deliver value, create curiosity, or feel good to watch.

## 1. The first 1–2 seconds decide everything
- Open on the most intriguing line, number or visual — not on "So…", "Hi guys", or setup. Cut the warm-up.
- Hook text on screen in the first frame (people watch muted). Big, short (2–6 words), one keyword visually dominant.
- Strong hook types: a surprising number ("₹500 every month"), a contrarian claim, a relatable pain ("agar tum bhi confused ho…"), a time/place oddity ("raat ke 3:45 ka idea"), a direct question, or a result shown first.
- If the best line is in the middle, it may be moved to the front as a cold open (ask/confirm for talking-head footage).

## 2. Remove every reason to skip
- Dead air, breaths between sentences, "umm/matlab/basically", retakes, repeated points, long setups → cut.
- Each sentence should earn its place; if the reel works without it, remove it.
- Target length: the shortest version that keeps the full payoff. 20–45s is the sweet spot for talking-head; longer only if every beat is strong.

## 3. Pattern interrupts every 2–4 seconds
- Something visual changes regularly: zoom level on jump cuts, a punch-in on the key word, a caption style beat, a meme card, a B-roll, a transition at a new section.
- But **restraint**: one main effect per moment. Too many effects = noise, and the viewer stops reading.

## 4. Open loops and payoff
- Tease what's coming ("3 steps…", "last one is the best") so viewers stay to get it.
- Deliver the payoff clearly, then end fast. No long outros.
- Ending that loops back into the start makes rewatches (counts as more watch time).

## 5. Captions are the main visual
- Most people watch muted: captions must be readable in under a second — 2–4 words per group, high contrast, safe zone above the bottom UI.
- Highlight the one word that carries the meaning (number, emotion, action). Never duplicate captions with pop-ups.
- Style follows topic and mood; consistent within one reel.

## 6. Sound design = emotion
- Meme sounds/reactions land jokes and reactions; use them where the speaker's tone invites it (question, fail, flex, shock).
- Music bed low and ducked under voice; voice always clear and loud (-14 LUFS).
- Swooshes only for real movement; silence is also a tool.

## 7. Culture & trend
- Indian audience: desi meme references (CID, Bollywood dialogues, current viral sounds) feel native and boost shares.
- Trends age fast: refresh the meme library regularly; keep classics for reliability.

## 8. Checklist before export
- [ ] Hook in frame 1, readable muted
- [ ] No dead air > 0.3s, no retakes, no filler
- [ ] A visual change at least every 4s
- [ ] Nothing shown twice on screen; nothing overlaps the face's eyes/mouth
- [ ] Captions correct (Hinglish), keyword highlighted
- [ ] Sound: voice clear, effects tasteful, no abrupt clicks
- [ ] Ends right after the payoff
